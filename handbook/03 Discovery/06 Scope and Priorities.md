# Scope and Priorities

> Part of the **[Discovery](README.md)**.

## Purpose

Scope and priorities define what should be addressed, what should be deferred and what matters most.

They help teams focus delivery on the work that best supports the desired outcomes.

## Guidance

Discovery often reveals more needs than can be addressed at once. Scope and prioritisation turn understanding into a realistic delivery direction.

Good prioritisation is not only about importance. It also considers value, risk, dependency, urgency, effort and learning.

### Define Scope Boundaries

Scope should describe what is included and what is excluded.

Explicit boundaries reduce misunderstanding and help prevent uncontrolled expansion of work.

### Prioritise by Outcome

Priorities should be linked to goals and outcomes.

Work that does not support the desired outcome should be challenged, deferred or removed.

### Consider Risk and Learning

High-risk or high-uncertainty areas may need early attention.

Sometimes the most valuable early work is not the largest feature, but the work that answers an important question or reduces significant uncertainty.

### Respect Dependencies

Priorities should account for dependencies between concepts, decisions, teams, systems and delivery steps.

Ignoring dependencies can create blocked work or force avoidable rework.

### Keep Scope Negotiable

Scope should remain open to adjustment as understanding improves.

Changing scope is not failure when it reflects better evidence, clearer priorities or more accurate understanding.

## Key Takeaways

- Scope defines what is included and excluded.
- Priorities should support desired outcomes.
- Risk, learning and dependency should influence prioritisation.
- Not all discovered needs should be delivered immediately.
- Scope should adapt as understanding improves.
