# Context Mapping

> Part of the **[Domain Modelling](README.md)**.

## Purpose

Context mapping identifies the major areas of the domain and how they relate to one another.

It helps teams understand where language, ownership, responsibility and integration boundaries may exist.

## Guidance

Large domains rarely have one single model that works everywhere. Different parts of the business may use different language, rules and priorities.

Context mapping makes these differences visible before teams design solution boundaries.

### Identify Domain Areas

Teams should identify the major areas of business capability, responsibility or workflow.

These areas provide the starting point for understanding where separate models may be needed.

### Look for Language Boundaries

Different meanings for the same term often reveal context boundaries.

If a term changes meaning between teams, processes or workflows, that difference should be explored rather than smoothed over.

### Understand Relationships

Context maps should show how domain areas depend on, support or exchange information with one another.

The goal is to understand collaboration and dependency, not to design system integration prematurely.

### Capture Ownership

Ownership helps clarify who understands, governs or changes a domain area.

Unclear ownership can lead to weak models, conflicting rules and difficult decisions later.

### Keep the Map Useful

A context map should support conversation and decision-making.

It does not need to be visually perfect. It needs to help the team reason about boundaries and relationships.

## Key Takeaways

- Context mapping reveals major domain areas and relationships.
- Different language can indicate different contexts.
- Ownership and dependencies should be visible.
- Context maps support modelling before technical design.
- The map should be useful, not decorative.
