# Decision Criteria

> Part of the **[Decisions](README.md)**.

## Purpose

Decision criteria define how options should be compared before a significant decision is made.

They help teams make choices based on relevant factors rather than preference alone.

## Guidance

Decision criteria should be appropriate to the decision being made.

The goal is not to create bureaucracy, but to make trade-offs visible enough that decisions can be challenged and understood.

### Align with Goals

Criteria should reflect the goals and outcomes discovered earlier in the lifecycle.

A decision that does not support the desired outcome should be questioned.

### Consider Constraints

Constraints may limit which options are realistic.

Time, cost, regulation, security, team capability, existing systems and operational maturity can all influence the decision.

### Evaluate Trade-offs

Options should be compared by what they improve and what they make harder.

Trade-offs may involve complexity, speed, reliability, maintainability, flexibility, cost or risk.

### Include Long-Term Impact

Decisions should consider future maintenance, operations and evolution.

A fast option may still be the right choice, but the future cost should be understood.

### Use Evidence Where Possible

Evidence may include prototypes, measurements, prior experience, research, incidents, examples or expert input.

Where evidence is incomplete, uncertainty should be stated clearly.

## Key Takeaways

- Criteria make decision-making more transparent.
- Decisions should align with goals and outcomes.
- Constraints affect realistic options.
- Trade-offs should be explicit.
- Long-term impact should be considered.
- Evidence should guide judgement where possible.
