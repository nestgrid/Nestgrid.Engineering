# Goals and Outcomes

> Part of the **[Discovery](README.md)**.

## Purpose

Goals and outcomes define what success should look like and why the work is worth doing.

They connect discovery to measurable value and help teams evaluate whether a solution is moving in the right direction.

## Guidance

Goals describe intended direction. Outcomes describe meaningful changes that should result from the work.

Clear goals and outcomes help teams prioritise, make trade-offs and avoid confusing delivery activity with success.

### Define the Desired Change

An outcome should describe the change expected after the problem is addressed.

This may involve improved efficiency, reduced risk, better visibility, increased reliability, improved user experience or stronger compliance.

### Distinguish Outputs from Outcomes

An output is something delivered. An outcome is the effect it creates.

For example, a feature, document or service may be an output. The improved decision-making, reduced manual work or lower operational risk is the outcome.

### Make Success Observable

Outcomes should be observable where practical.

Teams should be able to recognise whether progress is being made, even when measurement is qualitative rather than numerical.

### Align with Stakeholders

Goals should be agreed with the people who are accountable for the outcome.

Misaligned goals create conflicting priorities and make later decisions harder.

### Keep Goals Focused

Discovery should avoid too many competing goals.

Focused goals make it easier to prioritise scope, resolve trade-offs and evaluate whether the work remains on track.

## Key Takeaways

- Goals define direction; outcomes define meaningful change.
- Delivery outputs should not be confused with success.
- Outcomes should be observable where practical.
- Goals should be aligned with accountable stakeholders.
- Focused goals improve prioritisation and decision-making.
