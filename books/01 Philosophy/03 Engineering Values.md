# Engineering Values

> Part of the **[Philosophy](README.md)**.

## Purpose

Engineering values define the behaviours and professional standards expected when applying the engineering philosophy.

Principles guide decisions. Values guide conduct. Together, they help engineers collaborate effectively, make responsible choices and deliver software that remains valuable beyond its initial release.

## Guidance

Engineering is a human discipline as much as a technical one. Good outcomes depend on clarity, ownership, humility and respect for the people affected by the software.

These values should influence how engineers communicate, design, review, test, document and operate systems.

### Clarity

Engineers should communicate ideas, decisions and trade-offs clearly.

Clear writing, clear naming and clear design reduce misunderstanding and make software easier to maintain.

### Ownership

Engineers should take responsibility for the quality, reliability and maintainability of the systems they help create.

Ownership does not end when code is merged. It includes documentation, testing, deployment, operations and continuous improvement.

### Integrity

Engineers should communicate honestly about risks, assumptions and limitations.

Problems should be surfaced early rather than hidden. Decisions, estimates and trade-offs should be transparent, allowing teams and stakeholders to make informed choices.

### Discipline

Engineering work should be deliberate and consistent.

Disciplined engineers follow agreed standards, avoid unnecessary shortcuts and make trade-offs explicit when constraints require compromise.

### Humility

Engineers should recognise that understanding grows over time.

Good engineering requires listening to domain experts, learning from users, accepting feedback and revising decisions when better evidence emerges.

### Collaboration

Software is rarely built well in isolation.

Engineers should share knowledge, review work constructively and help create an environment where the best ideas can come from anywhere.

### Stewardship

Engineers should care for the long-term health of the systems they build, they are the custodians of these systems.

This includes reducing avoidable complexity, improving documentation, paying attention to operational concerns and leaving systems easier to understand than they were found.

## Key Takeaways

- Values guide engineering behaviour and professional judgement.
- Clarity improves communication, design and maintainability.
- Ownership extends beyond writing and merging code.
- Discipline keeps engineering work consistent and trustworthy.
- Humility helps teams learn and improve.
- Stewardship protects the long-term health of software systems.
