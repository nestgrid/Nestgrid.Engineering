# Decision Records

> Part of the **[Decisions](README.md)**.

## Purpose

Decision records capture significant decisions and the reasoning behind them.

They help future readers understand the context, options, trade-offs and consequences of choices made during the lifecycle.

## Guidance

A decision record should be short enough to read but complete enough to explain the decision.

It should preserve the thinking that would otherwise be lost in meetings, messages or memory.

### Record Significant Decisions

Not every decision needs a formal record.

Record decisions that affect direction, scope, architecture, technology, data, security, operations, cost, risk or long-term maintainability.

### Capture Context

The record should explain the situation that made the decision necessary.

Context helps future engineers understand what was known at the time and why the decision made sense.

### Explain the Rationale

A decision should include why the selected option was chosen.

The rationale should mention important trade-offs, constraints, risks and alternatives where relevant.

### Describe Consequences

Every decision creates consequences.

The record should describe expected benefits, accepted costs and known follow-up work.

### Keep Records Discoverable

Decision records should use consistent naming, numbering and location.

If decisions are hard to find, they will not be used.

## Key Takeaways

- Decision records preserve reasoning, not just outcomes.
- Significant decisions should be recorded.
- Context and rationale make decisions understandable.
- Consequences should be explicit.
- Consistent organisation keeps decisions useful.
