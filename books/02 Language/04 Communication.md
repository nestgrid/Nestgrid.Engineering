# Communication

> Part of the **[Language](README.md)**.

## Purpose

Communication defines how shared language is used in conversations, documentation, decisions and collaboration.

Clear communication helps teams discover the right problem, explain trade-offs and preserve understanding throughout the engineering lifecycle.

## Guidance

Engineering communication should reduce ambiguity and improve shared understanding. It should help people make better decisions, not merely exchange information.

The quality of communication directly affects the quality of discovery, modelling, architecture and implementation.

### Use Plain Language

Communication should be understandable to the intended audience.

Technical terms are useful when they add precision, but they should not be used to make simple ideas harder to understand.

### State Assumptions

Assumptions should be made visible.

When a decision depends on an assumption, the assumption should be documented or discussed so it can be challenged, validated or revised.

### Explain Trade-offs

Engineering communication should include the reasoning behind important decisions.

It is not enough to say what was chosen. Teams should understand why it was chosen and what alternatives or consequences were considered.

### Ask Clarifying Questions

Unclear language should be clarified early.

Questions about meaning, scope, rules or intent should be treated as valuable engineering work, not interruptions.

### Keep Communication Traceable

Important understanding should move from conversation into durable artefacts.

Decisions, definitions, rules and constraints should not live only in memory or private messages.

## Key Takeaways

- Communication should reduce ambiguity and improve understanding.
- Plain language should be preferred where possible.
- Assumptions should be visible and testable.
- Important decisions should include reasoning and trade-offs.
- Clarifying questions are part of good engineering.
- Important knowledge should be captured in durable artefacts.
