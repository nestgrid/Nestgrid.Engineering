# Product Designer

> Canonical role for product experience, interaction and usability design.

## Purpose

The Product Designer turns product intent and user understanding into coherent, usable and accessible experiences.

This role applies to products with user journeys, interfaces, workflows or other experience surfaces. It may be performed by a human, agent or mixed team.

## Responsibilities

- Understand user goals, contexts, journeys and constraints.
- Define information architecture and interaction flows.
- Explore and communicate experience options through appropriate prototypes or other representations.
- Identify usability, accessibility and consistency risks early.
- Contribute to product scope, requirements and acceptance criteria where experience decisions affect them.
- Establish or extend design-system guidance where it materially improves consistency.
- Explain experience decisions, assumptions and trade-offs in durable artefacts.
- Prepare clear handovers for Architecture, Engineering, Quality and Documentation where their work is affected.

## Authority

The Product Designer may recommend experience direction and define experience requirements within approved product intent.

The role does not approve product scope, override architecture, replace accessibility or security validation, or make release decisions owned by the Project Sponsor.

Material changes to approved product intent or scope must be escalated to the Product Owner and Project Sponsor through the applicable workflow.

## Typical Inputs

- Product Brief
- User and stakeholder understanding
- Business capabilities and requirements
- Existing product research and feedback
- Architecture and technical constraints

## Typical Outputs

- User journeys and interaction flows
- Information architecture
- Prototypes or experience specifications
- Accessibility and usability requirements
- Design decisions and rationale
- Experience handover notes

## Working Process

1. Read the approved product intent, relevant workflow and existing experience evidence.
2. Clarify uncertainty with the Product Owner and affected roles before narrowing the solution.
3. Explore alternatives and make user, accessibility and product trade-offs visible.
4. Recommend an experience direction appropriate to the product's stage and risk.
5. Produce the required experience artefacts and route them to the next responsible role.
6. Collaborate with Engineering and Quality to keep the implemented experience aligned with the approved direction.

## Definition of Done

The Product Designer is ready to hand over when:

- the relevant user journeys and experience requirements are understood;
- important alternatives, assumptions and constraints are recorded;
- accessibility and usability considerations are visible;
- affected downstream roles can act without reconstructing the design discussion; and
- the handover identifies the next responsible role and required action.
