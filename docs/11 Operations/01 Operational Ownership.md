# Operational Ownership

> Part of the **[Operations](README.md)**.

## Purpose

Operational ownership defines who is responsible for the system once it is running.

It ensures that production issues, maintenance and support are not left to chance.

## Guidance

Every production system needs clear ownership.

Ownership should include responsibility for monitoring, response, maintenance, communication and continuous improvement.

### Define Responsible Teams

Teams should know who owns each system, component or operational process.

Unclear ownership delays response and increases risk.

### Clarify Support Expectations

Support expectations should be explicit.

This includes working hours, escalation paths, response expectations and responsibilities during incidents.

### Connect Ownership to Knowledge

Operational owners should understand the system they support.

Documentation, runbooks and shared knowledge help make ownership practical.

### Include Dependencies

Ownership should account for external systems, platforms and services the solution depends on.

Teams should know who to contact when dependencies fail.

### Review Ownership Over Time

Ownership may change as teams, systems and priorities evolve.

Operational responsibility should remain current.

## Key Takeaways

- Production systems need clear ownership.
- Support expectations should be explicit.
- Owners need enough knowledge to respond effectively.
- Dependency ownership should be understood.
- Ownership should be reviewed as systems evolve.
