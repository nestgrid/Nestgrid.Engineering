# Monitoring and Alerting

> Part of the **[Operations](README.md)**.

## Purpose

Monitoring and alerting define how teams detect unhealthy behaviour and know when action is required.

They help reduce time to awareness and support reliable operation.

## Guidance

Monitoring should focus on symptoms that matter to users, business outcomes and operational health.

Alerts should be actionable. An alert that nobody can respond to becomes noise.

### Monitor User Impact

Monitoring should include signals that reflect user or business impact.

Internal technical metrics are useful, but they should connect to meaningful service health.

### Define Healthy Behaviour

Teams should know what normal behaviour looks like.

Without a baseline, it is difficult to distinguish real problems from harmless variation.

### Make Alerts Actionable

An alert should indicate that someone needs to investigate or act.

Alerts should include enough context to begin diagnosis.

### Avoid Alert Fatigue

Too many noisy alerts reduce responsiveness.

Low-value alerts should be removed, tuned or converted into dashboards.

### Review After Incidents

Incidents should lead to monitoring improvements where gaps are found.

Missed, late or noisy alerts are signals that monitoring needs refinement.

## Key Takeaways

- Monitoring should reflect meaningful health.
- Alerts should be actionable.
- Teams need a baseline for normal behaviour.
- Alert fatigue reduces operational effectiveness.
- Incidents should improve monitoring quality.
