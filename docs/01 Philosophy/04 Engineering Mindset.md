# Engineering Mindset

> Part of the **[Philosophy](README.md)**.

## Purpose

Engineering mindset describes how engineers approach problems, uncertainty and responsibility throughout the software lifecycle.

It connects philosophy to everyday judgement by encouraging engineers to think critically, reason from evidence and make decisions that remain understandable over time.

## Guidance

Good engineering requires more than technical skill. It requires a way of thinking that balances business value, domain understanding, technical quality and operational reality.

The right mindset helps engineers avoid premature solutions, unnecessary complexity and decisions that cannot be explained or maintained.

### Start with Understanding

Engineers should seek to understand the problem before proposing a solution.

This includes the business context, user needs, constraints, risks and existing system behaviour. Assumptions should be made visible and validated where possible.

### Think in Trade-offs

Most engineering decisions involve trade-offs.

A good decision is not always the most technically elegant option. It is the option that best balances value, cost, risk, complexity and maintainability in the current context.

### Prefer Evidence Over Opinion

Engineering judgement should be informed by facts, examples, measurements and experience.

Opinions are useful, but they should be tested against evidence. When evidence is incomplete, the uncertainty should be acknowledged rather than hidden.

### Optimise for Maintainability

Software is read, changed and operated many times after it is first written.

Engineers should favour clear structure, explicit decisions, readable code and useful documentation over cleverness that only works in the moment.

### Reduce Unnecessary Complexity

Complexity is sometimes required, but it should never be added casually.

Before introducing a pattern, abstraction, dependency or process, engineers should understand the problem it solves and the cost it introduces.

### Learn Continuously

Engineering knowledge evolves as products, teams and technologies change.

Engineers should remain open to feedback, review their own decisions and improve practices when better approaches become available.

## Key Takeaways

- Engineering mindset shapes how engineers reason, not just what they produce.
- Understanding should come before solution design.
- Good decisions balance trade-offs in context.
- Evidence should guide judgement wherever possible.
- Maintainability is a core engineering concern.
- Complexity should be deliberate and justified.
