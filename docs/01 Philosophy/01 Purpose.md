# Purpose

> Part of the **[Philosophy](README.md)**.

## Purpose

Engineering is the disciplined process of understanding problems, designing effective solutions and delivering software that creates lasting value.

The objective is not simply to write code, but to solve meaningful problems through thoughtful analysis, sound design and continuous improvement.

Software is the outcome of engineering—not its purpose.

## Guidance

Engineering should always begin by understanding the problem before considering possible solutions.

Successful engineers recognise that technology exists to serve business and user needs. Every design decision, implementation choice and optimisation should contribute towards solving a clearly understood problem.

Engineering also extends beyond initial delivery. Software should remain maintainable, adaptable and operable throughout its lifetime.

### Business First

Every engineering effort should begin with understanding:

- Why the problem exists.
- Who is affected.
- What outcome is expected.
- What constraints must be respected.

Technology should never become the driving force behind the solution.

### Long-Term Thinking

Engineering decisions should consider not only today's requirements but also future maintenance, scalability and evolution.

Short-term convenience should not compromise long-term sustainability without a deliberate and justified trade-off.

### Continuous Improvement

Engineering is an iterative discipline.

As knowledge grows, engineers should continuously refine their understanding, improve designs and simplify solutions while preserving correctness and business value.

## Key Takeaways

- Engineering exists to solve problems, not simply to write software.
- Business understanding should precede technical implementation.
- Technology is a means to achieve an outcome, not the outcome itself.
- Good engineering balances immediate needs with long-term sustainability.
- Continuous improvement is an essential engineering responsibility.
