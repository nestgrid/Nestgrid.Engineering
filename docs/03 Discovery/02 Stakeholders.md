# Stakeholders

> Part of the **[Discovery](README.md)**.

## Purpose

Stakeholder discovery identifies the people, teams and groups affected by the problem or involved in the solution.

It ensures that requirements, constraints and decisions are informed by the right perspectives.

## Guidance

Stakeholders are not limited to sponsors or decision-makers. They include people who use, operate, support, govern, fund, depend on or are affected by the system.

Missing an important stakeholder can lead to incomplete requirements, poor adoption, operational gaps or avoidable rework.

### Identify Stakeholder Groups

Teams should identify the groups connected to the problem and outcome.

This may include users, business owners, operators, support teams, compliance representatives, security teams, customers, partners and engineering teams.

### Understand Responsibilities

Each stakeholder group should be understood in terms of its role, responsibilities and concerns.

Knowing what a group is accountable for helps explain why certain requirements or constraints matter.

### Capture Needs and Concerns

Stakeholders may care about different outcomes.

Some may prioritise usability, others reliability, auditability, cost, speed, security, maintainability or operational simplicity.

### Recognise Decision Authority

Discovery should clarify who can make decisions and who must be consulted.

Confusion about authority can delay delivery or cause decisions to be revisited late in the lifecycle.

### Keep Engagement Proportional

Not every stakeholder needs the same level of involvement.

The level of engagement should reflect the stakeholder's influence, impact, expertise and dependency on the outcome.

## Key Takeaways

- Stakeholders include everyone meaningfully affected by the problem or solution.
- Different stakeholders may have different needs and concerns.
- Decision authority should be clear.
- Missing stakeholders can create late rework and poor outcomes.
- Engagement should be intentional and proportionate.
