# Constraints and Risks

> Part of the **[Discovery](README.md)**.

## Purpose

Constraints and risks define the limits, uncertainties and potential problems that may shape the solution or affect delivery.

They help teams make informed decisions before committing to design, scope or implementation.

## Guidance

Discovery should make constraints and risks visible early. Hidden constraints often become expensive surprises later in the lifecycle.

Constraints describe limits that must be respected. Risks describe uncertain events or conditions that could affect success.

### Identify Constraints

Constraints may be business, technical, regulatory, financial, operational, security-related, organisational or time-based.

They should be captured clearly because they influence requirements, architecture, scope and delivery strategy.

### Separate Constraints from Preferences

Not every preference is a constraint.

Teams should distinguish between what must be true and what is merely preferred. This prevents unnecessary limitation of design options.

### Capture Assumptions

Assumptions should be documented when decisions depend on something not yet proven.

Unvalidated assumptions should be treated as potential risks until they are confirmed or replaced with evidence.

### Assess Risk Impact

Risks should be understood in terms of likelihood, impact and possible response.

The goal is not to eliminate all risk, but to make risk visible enough to manage responsibly.

### Review Risks Continuously

Risks change as understanding improves.

Discovery risks may be reduced, accepted, transferred, mitigated or escalated as the work progresses.

## Key Takeaways

- Constraints define limits that must be respected.
- Risks define uncertainty that may affect success.
- Preferences should not be mistaken for constraints.
- Assumptions should be visible and validated where possible.
- Risks should be assessed and reviewed continuously.
